\newcommand{\papertitle}{From fatty acids to politics: how a seed-oil community on Reddit changed topic in 2024 (working title)}
\newcommand{\paperkeywords}{seed oils $|$ Reddit $|$ topic models $|$ BERTopic}
\newcommand{\abstracttext}{The Reddit community built around claims that seed oils are harmful grew about threefold in 2024. Using 149{,}815 comments and self-posts from r/StopEatingSeedOils (collected 22 November 2026), we ask what the community discussed before and after the surge. A BERTopic model found 123 topics, which we grouped by hand into 14 themes. Politics rose from 0.65\% to 3.21\% of themed documents ($\times$4.9; $\times$3.6--5.4 under other outlier rules) and vaccines from 0.32\% to 1.14\% ($\times$3.5), while fatty-acid biochemistry fell from 19.1\% to 12.8\% ($\times$0.67). Politics peaked in late 2024 (7.2\% from August to December) and fell back to 1.1\% in 2026. Directions held under four outlier rules; a hand check of 90 comments showed the outlier rule improves topic fit only for politics. Themes are hand-made design choices, and we do not identify what caused the surge.}

\newcommand{\significancetext}{When an online health community suddenly grows, what its members talk about can change as much as how many there are. In one large Reddit community about seed oils, talk of fatty-acid chemistry gave way in 2024 to politics, vaccines and distrust of industry, and the political part faded within a year. We show how to track such shifts without labelled data, using a topic model grouped by hand and checked by reading samples.}
