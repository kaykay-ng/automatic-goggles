In other words, the EU has set itself the objective of having an economy with zero net greenhouse gas emissions.
This goal is at the heart of the European Green Agreement and is in line with the EU's commitment to increase global climate action in line with the commitments of the Paris Agreement.
The Paris Agreement, adopted in 2015 by the parties to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change and ratified by Spain in 2017, establishes, in its Article 2, as a limit of global warming: “to keep the global average temperature rise below 2°C compared to pre-industrial levels, and to continue efforts to limit that temperature increase to 1.5°C with respect to pre-industrial levels”.
In turn, Article 4 demands that: “All parties should strive to formulate and communicate long-term strategies for low-greenhouse gas development, bearing in mind Article 2 and taking into account their common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities, in the light of different national circumstances”.
In order to implement these and other commitments within the Union, the European Commission proposed at the end of 2016 the package of measures called “Clean Energy for All Europeans”.
This package includes Regulation 2018/1999 on the governance of the Energy Union and Climate Action, which sets out the need for the development of long-term strategies by Member States, with a perspective of at least 30 years.
The EU, as such, will present its own long-term strategy to the United Nations, based on national proposals.
This is an action in line with the latest available scientific knowledge: the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) published its special report on the impact of global warming of 1.5 °C above pre-industrial levels in October 2018.
The authors concluded that, if international climate action was not intensified, the rise of the global average temperature could reach 2 °C shortly after 2060 and continue to increase thereafter.
That the EU, in line with scientific evidence, boost climate neutrality in 2050 implies a commitment to lead climate action globally.
Spain is aligned with this European commitment, as formally agreed in the conclusions of the European Council of 12 December 2019, endorsing the objective of making the EU climate neutral by 2050.
A commitment that has also been demonstrated at the national level, with the Declaration of Climate and Environmental Emergency agreed by the Council of Ministers on 21 January 2020, a resolution that responds to the widespread consensus of the scientific community, calling for urgent action to safeguard the environment, health and security of citizens.
Our country is in an area of special vulnerability to the impacts of climate change.
Increased temperatures, changes in rainfall or droughts can have serious effects on sectors such as agriculture, livestock, forestry or tourism, as well as negative impacts on the health of the population, mainly on the population at risk of exclusion.
Spain is in an area of special vulnerability to the impacts of climate change.
Combating it is a matter of responsibility with the present and the future.

It is also an obligation in line with the commitments it has undertaken as an EU Member State and part of the Paris Agreement.
And it is the only and best way to attend to the call to climate action of Science and the whole of citizenship.
In line with this, Spain has to implement policies aimed at achieving carbon neutrality by 2050.
The development of strategies provides the basis for the country to take advantage of the multitude of opportunities that will emerge on this path to climate neutrality.
Likewise, the availability of a less fossil-fuel-dependent energy system will reduce the country's exposure to variations in international fuel markets, thereby increasing its resilience to international changes and its competitiveness in the global context.
Therefore, the objective of this Long-term Strategy (hereinafter ELP or Strategy) is to articulate a coherent and integrated response to the climate crisis, which takes advantage of the opportunities for the modernization and competitiveness of our economy and is socially fair and inclusive.
It is a road map to move towards climate neutrality by 20501, with intermediate milestones in 2030 and 2040.
Due to its time frame, it should be noted that the mission of the ELP is not to know how specific technological changes will occur in different sectors of the economy.
The aim is to present a coherent proposal on this transformation using the scientific and technical knowledge currently available.
In the coming decades, not only technological innovations will take place, but also transformations in economic systems or forms of social organization, which are difficult to anticipate with certainty.
However, the threats and risks of climate change are well known, as are many ways of preventing them.
In this way, the Strategy provides medium- and long-term signals to investors, economic, social and environmental actors, and to Spanish society as a whole, for the period 2021-2050.
In this sense, it has a threefold objective:

Second, anticipate and plan the transition to a climate neutral economy, taking into account challenges and social, business and political debate about its implications and needs.
This transformation has to be addressed in a comprehensive manner, as it affects many cross-cutting elements of the economy and society. Third, it offers a clear objective in the long term, which will help anticipate the necessary lines of action and therefore maximize and exploit the opportunities arising from the energy transition by reducing risks.
6 This strategy is built on the basis of technological neutrality.
In principle, different technologies must have the same opportunities to develop in order to offer the best cost-efficient solutions for the de-carbonisation of each sector of the economy, however, the set of positive and negative externalities that accompany each technology and its respective degree of maturity must be taken into account.
Knowing the different challenges to achieving climate neutrality allows us to anticipate the needs for investment in research, development and innovation that must be put in place in order for technologies to be ready in the coming years.
It is also possible to design strategies to achieve leadership positions in the development of these innovations, which constitute an important opportunity for generating employment and business lines, thus advancing in the strengthening of the R & D & I system and the competitiveness of the Spanish economy.
All this, in a context of greater and better protection of biodiversity, greater resilience in adaptation to climate change, more efficient use of resources and the design of more habitable cities with better air quality.

This project institutionalizes the elaboration of the Long-term Strategy, the NIPEC and the NAPCC, and establishes the necessary coherence among all tools, as there is no other way to ensure, in a reliable, inclusive, transparent and predictable manner, the achievement of the 2030 and long-term goals and targets.
This means that the ELP must project a path consistent with the decarbonisation objectives of the economy by 2050, and with the actions planned for 2030, which will require the mobilization of different administrations and private actors.
An integrated governance system for the NIP and the ELP will be implemented based on indicators and aligned with the monitoring reports to be developed under the Governance Regulation of the NIP 2021-20302.
The objective is to monitor the goals set out in both documents.
In this way, it will be possible to identify difficulties and challenges, as well as react in advance and flexibility.
The aim of this transparency exercise is also to encourage the involvement and support of citizens.
The crucial element for success will be the social, political and business support that transformation generates, as it is a structural change in the medium and long term that will cover many political and economic cycles.
Within the framework of this governance system, coordination, collaboration and involvement of the autonomous communities, as well as local authorities, will be essential to achieve the objectives.
A large part of the powers required to carry it out belong to these entities.
As envisaged in the Governance Regulation, the Long-term Strategy is a flexible instrument to guide economic and energy transformation towards climate neutrality in the mid-century.
The specific route for each decade will be defined in detail through the NIPs, which will be developed every ten years and updated every five years.
In the same vein, the Strategy will be updated every five years with the latest information available, so that the specific trajectories of sectors and technologies will vary.
Through the ELP and the PNIEC, the average (2030) and the long term (2050) are integrated, providing credibility, climate commitment and industrial opportunities, as part of a single strategic vision for Spain.
